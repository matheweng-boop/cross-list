How a preliminary case is built

What we learnt building the car-radio case, and the template every preliminary case follows, so cases can be compared side by side. The template file is data/business/cases/_template.yaml.

What we learnt

#LessonWhat went wrong firstRule now
1Start from the architecture, not the datasheetAdvantages were listed per chipCompare where our blocks sit in the library block diagrams against each competitor, and where the architecture is going (zonal, central compute)
2Check what we already have“Digital-output tuner” was proposed, but every Si479xx already outputs I/QBefore proposing a feature, confirm it isn't in today's parts or a firmware away
3A roadmap item is a real stepA new interface or a quad in one package was a tweakA roadmap item changes what the system can do or where it can sit; tweaks become supporting actions
4State disadvantages as well as advantagesOnly strengths were listedFor each competitor: where we lose (cost, integration, ecosystem) and how we make up for it
5Respect competitors' cost positionChip-for-chip comparison against STMature, reused designs win on cost per chip; compete on system cost or capability instead
6Be specific about the deviceVague “tuner family”Name it, list what it reuses, what is new, its outputs, what it replaces and which competitor part it answers
7Score the distance from todayEffort was implicitRate every feature 0–4 (below) against the constraint, e.g. “reuse only, 2–3 years”
8Separate data from estimatesClaims mixed with numbersEvery figure has a source and a confidence; every forecast is marked “est” with its basis
9Argue both sidesTrends were one-sidedFor each trend (e.g. software radio, remote tuner modules, HD Radio): what speaks for, what against, and what it does to our product
10Price from anchors and valueNo pricePublic anchors → value of what the part replaces → entry / target / premium; real ASPs replace the estimates
11Attach-rate levers need evidence“Open the evaluation kit” was overstatedEach lever cites what competitors do or what customers say; otherwise it is marked as judgement
12Don't guess the expected answer—Build from evidence, mark gaps (e.g. RF performance, die cost need internal data), and let feedback adjust the case
13State the customer's architectural benefitCases said what we would build, not what changes in the customer's systemBefore and after in the customer's architecture, then each gain with its type (fewer parts, cabling, placement, scaling, performance, safety, software, sourcing, compliance), how it is achieved and a measure; and what the customer gives up

The process

  1. Question and constraint: what to decide, time frame, reuse limits.
  2. Architecture: library block diagrams for us and each competitor; architecture trend.
  3. Customer benefit: what changes in the customer's system, today against with the proposal, each gain typed and measured.
  4. Position: advantages, disadvantages (with offset), differentiators good and bad.
  5. Market: today's figures with sources; where it is heading as estimates with basis; for/against per trend.
  6. Features: what could benefit us, each with its distance from today.
  7. Product: one specific definition built from the strongest features.
  8. ASP: anchors, value, range; real values when known.
  9. Customer block diagram: the customer's system with the proposal in place, ready to edit and show.
  10. Questions to ask: to the business unit and to OEMs / Tier-1s, for what public data cannot settle.
  11. Attach rate and risks.
  12. Feedback loop: reviewers agree, disagree or move a distance closer or further on each item; the case is revised.
