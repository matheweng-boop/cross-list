The idea in five steps

  1. Collect systems. Public block diagrams of end systems (applications) from TI, NXP, Qualcomm, MediaTek, Skyworks and others.
  2. Normalize. Every block becomes a function (a chip type) with a quantity: a block on the board.
  3. Match. For each block: do Skyworks / Qorvo have a part (Portfolio), nobody (Gap), only competitors (Competitor only), or is it a chip we do not make (Customer part)?
  4. Score. One Opportunity score 0–100 per function: demand, distance from our portfolio, architecture fit, competition and market trend, each with a confidence.
  5. Decide. Rankings point at candidates; a preliminary case or a Case analysis collects the facts for one candidate.

The path to product is used where assessed, else 0 for a function we already sell, else the technology rating. A curated automotive row may set its own distance (e.g. 2 for a package from our dies). Preliminary case features use the same scale.

Confidence

  • Missing data scores the neutral {{ cfg.neutral }} with low confidence: an unresearched function is not punished as a bad one. Low-confidence factors are listed on Data quality as research to do; a low overall confidence is flagged ⚑.
  • Overall confidence = the lowest of {{ cfg.confidence.overall_from|map('capitalize')|join(', ') }}. Architecture fit and trend are shown but do not set it.
  • Reviews: a reviewer's latest “wrong” on the data behind a factor (path, technology rating, competition, trend) lowers that factor's confidence one step (high → medium → low); “correct” changes nothing. On a preliminary case, a “disagree” lowers that item's confidence one step and the case's confidence one step.

What the analysis cannot tell you

  • Specs are not checked. "Portfolio" means a candidate part exists for the function, not that it meets the application's voltage, current, band or qualification.
  • No sales data. Attach and door openers are modeled from diagrams; they are not purchase records or market share.
  • The sample shapes the result. The numbers describe the diagrams collected, not the whole market; an area with many diagrams (e.g. RF) weighs more.
  • Indicative ratings. Competition and technology proximity are readings of public information for discussion, not a roadmap or a market study.
  • Catalogue mapping. A part we sell that is not mapped to the right function shows up as a gap; "Same technology" gaps are worth checking first.
  • No money without inputs. Every block weighs the same until ASPs and volumes are entered.
