In brief

  1. Why 48 V: the same power at a quarter of the current, so thinner wires and a lighter harness, and room for bigger pumps, fans, blowers and compressors. Tesla's Cybertruck runs its whole low-voltage net at 48 V; mild hybrids add a 48 V net next to 12 V.
  2. What a 48 V drive needs: 80–100 V MOSFETs, a gate driver that survives the 60–70 V transients of the 48 V standards with margin (est), an MCU for 6-step or FOC, current sensing and LIN or CAN. Steering and brakes stay safety critical (ASIL C/D); pumps, fans, blowers, flaps and seat motors are comfort or thermal loads (QM to ASIL B, est).
  3. Competitors split it in two: a qualified 12 V motor-control MCU plus a separate 48 V gate driver (Infineon MOTIX TLE989x + TLE9140EQW, TI DRV8363-Q1, ST L9908, Allegro AMT49100, NXP GD3000). Only Elmos sells a one-chip 48 V controller (E523.52, 9–72 V).
  4. Ours fits the voltage and the integration: PAC5532AQX (25–160 V, Cortex-M4F, CAN controller, 2 A drivers) is a one-chip 48 V controller; ACT72350 (160 V gate driver with buck and sensing, host MCU outside) replaces up to 40 parts; PAC5527 (48 V absolute maximum) suits 12 V comfort motors only.
  5. What is missing is the grade: no PAC or ACT part has a stated AEC-Q100 grade in our data or in the public sources read, and no ISO 26262 process is stated. Answer: qualify one 160 V part and test it in one non-safety socket (est, low confidence).

What 48 V motor control needs

  • Voltage class: the 48 V net runs at about 36–52 V in normal use, with overvoltage near 60 V and short transients to about 70 V (est, as commonly quoted from VDA 320 / LV 148, not read). MOSFETs of 80–100 V and a gate driver of 80 V or more with margin: TI's DRV8363-Q1 runs 8–85 V, ST's L9908 4.5–75 V (95 V on the motor pins), Allegro's AMT49100 to 80 V, Infineon's TLE9140EQW 8–60 V (75 V absolute, 110 V robustness).
  • Gate drive: bootstrap plus a charge pump for 100 % duty; 1–2 A peak for 0.3–1 kW (TLE9140EQW: about 230 nC per MOSFET at 20 kHz; DRV8363-Q1: 1 A source, 2 A sink). See gate driver and bootstrap.
  • MCU: sensorless 6-step for fans and pumps, FOC for quiet blowers and compressors (Cortex-M3/M4 class). See BLDC with back-EMF.
  • Current and back-EMF sensing: one to three shunt amplifiers, phase comparators for the back-EMF zero crossing.
  • Vehicle side: LIN or CAN (FD), reverse-polarity protection, and often isolation between the 12 V control side and the 48 V power side (TI's 1 kW reference design isolates them).
  • Automotive grade: AEC-Q100 (Grade 1, −40 to 125 °C ambient, or Grade 0 next to the engine), PPAP and, from ASIL A up, an ISO 26262 process.

3. Gaps and new technology

  1. An AEC-Q100 version of PAC5532 or ACT72350. The single most valuable step: it turns the only one-chip 160 V controller of a broad-line vendor into an automotive part.

    part exists · evidence: catalogue, ACT72350 launch (Apr 2025), Infineon and TI two-chip 48 V offers.

  2. LIN and CAN FD on the controller. PAC5527 and PAC5532 have a CAN controller, not the transceiver; Melexis and Elmos put LIN on the motor chip.

    gap · evidence: Melexis MLX81350 news (snippet), Elmos flyer.

  3. An ISO 26262 safety package (safety manual, FMEDA) for ASIL A/B pumps and fans: Infineon's TLE9140EQW is ASIL B, ST's L9908 ASIL D, Allegro's AMT49100 is designed to ISO 26262.

    gap · evidence: vendor pages read.

  4. Isolation where 12 V meets 48 V. Our automotive Si86xx isolators and Si88xx isolated DC-DC fit the isolated CAN and supply of TI's 1 kW reference design; an attach for every isolated 48 V drive.

    automotive today · evidence: TIDUAY9 p.6 (isolated 12 V side), our catalogue.

  5. Power MOSFETs, transceivers and protection stay with partners.

    not ours

For the decision: Gap 1 decides everything; gaps 2 and 3 make the part competitive once it is qualified; gap 4 is an attach we can sell today.

4. Where else we see opportunities

  • Light electric vehicles (e-bikes, scooters, 2/3-wheelers, 48–72 V): not automotive, but the same 160 V controllers fit without qualification; ACT72350 and PAC5532 already name e-bikes and light EVs (launch and catalogue).
  • Trucks and buses (24 V): PAC5523/5524 (70–72 V) and PAC5532 cover 24 V nets with load-dump margin (est); Elmos and ST address 24 V too.
  • Robots, drones, power and garden tools (24–120 V): covered in the library diagrams for robot joints and drone ESCs; industrial grade is enough.
  • 48 V data-centre fans and pumps: server racks moved to 48 V; liquid-cooling pumps and fans need the same 48 V BLDC drive (est).

For the decision: The non-automotive 48 V markets can carry the same parts now, so an automotive version adds to a line that already sells.

Risks

  • The 48 V body net may stay rare outside mild hybrids and a few EVs; most comfort motors stay 12 V brushed.
  • Qualification cost and time (12–36 months) for a line that sells well in tools and light EVs without it.
  • The PAC5527 copy gives 105 °C ambient; Grade 1 at 125 °C may need a redesign (est).
  • Incumbents (Infineon, TI, ST, NXP) are in the thermal and body ECUs already and offer safety packages.
  • Qorvo data comes from our catalogue, a distributor-hosted PAC5527 copy and trade press; qorvo.com was not requested, so an automotive variant listed only there would be missed.

For the decision: Ask the BU for the grade first; spend nothing on a 48 V automotive push until one controller can be qualified and a Tier-1 asks for it.
