4. Pain points and numbers (our reading)

  • 4.1 · Inrush example: 500 µF at 800 V stores ½·500 µF·(800 V)² = 160 J; 800 V / 50 mΩ ≈ 16 kA first peak; with 50 Ω: 16 A peak, R·C = 25 ms, about 75 ms to 95 %; the resistor absorbs about the stored energy (160 J) per pre-charge · low (est; capacitance and loop resistance are typical assumptions, not from a source)
  • 4.2 · DC arcs (no current zero), contactor blow-out and gas-filled chambers, limited breaks under load, welding after inrush or short; HVIL with first-breaking pins · low (common engineering knowledge; not researched in this run)
  • 4.3 · Solid-state vs contactor (losses, microsecond fault turn-off, no galvanic gap, need for an isolated driver and sensing) · low (est)
  • 4.4 · 400 V class about 250–450 V, 800 V class about 550–900 V; fast chargers to 1000 V · low (common figures; not researched in this run)

7a. Data centre

  • 7.1 · Hot-swap history and the move to 800 V: 12 V era (power-limiting controllers: sense amplifier × FET voltage = FET power, limited to keep the FET in its SOA; then dV/dt start-up because bigger output capacitors made power limiting impractical); 12 V eFuses up to 60 A RMS, stackable, black-box fault recording, up to 4 kW per tray; 48 V era (5–6 kW per board; 12 V → 48 V cuts current by 75 % and I²R loss by 16×; GPU load steps 3–4× harsher); LM5066H2 dual gate drive (one strong-SOA FET for start-up and faults plus low-RDS(on) FETs for running, about 40 % smaller above 4 kW); TPS1685 named “the industry's first 48 V eFuse”; AI racks 100 kW to 1 MW, more than 20 kA at 48 V, so ±400 V (two- or three-wire) or 800 V; hot-swap on the high side, low side or both; a discrete 800 V hot-swap needs isolated gate drivers, current and voltage sense amplifiers, digital isolators, a multi-winding auxiliary supply and a microcontroller; LMI800PS integrates power and signal isolation and floats its ground so it works on either rail; 800 V FET choice is 1.2 kV SiC MOSFET (18 V gate) or SiC cascode JFET (12 V gate), in linear mode during inrush; power limit down to 200 W with timers; reinforced insulation, pollution degree 2, up to 3,050 m (5,000 m preferred) per IEC 62368-1; LMI800PS package ≥8 mm creepage, 5 kVrms, 10 kV surge; output capacitors discharged below 120 V within 2 s after unplugging · TI white paper STDA058 “Hot-Swap Solutions for High-Voltage DC-Powered AI Servers” (Oct 2026), cached copy from an earlier BizDev check, re-read 28 Oct 2026 · high — ti.com/lit/pdf/STDA058

9. Our reasoning for section 2b (est)

  • 9.1 · “Eight of twelve rows are the same circuit”: our count of the side-by-side table · low (est)
  • 9.2 · The car protects the wire, the rack protects the FET: from 7.1, 7.3, 7.5 (SOA ratings, power limit) vs 7.12, 7.13 (I²t curves) · med (est)
  • 9.3 · “About 60–70 % of the core” shared between a 48 V car eFuse driver and a 48–54 V rack hot-swap controller: our judgement from the shared blocks (sense amplifier, comparator, charge pump, gate control and timer, ADC and digital core) against the market-specific ones (SPI vs PMBus, AEC-Q100 / ASIL vs UL, sleep current vs high current); no design data · low (est)
  • 9.4 · A Si875x successor with 12–18 V drive covers both SiC MOSFETs (18 V) and SiC cascode JFETs (12 V); today's 8–12 V suits only the latter: from 2.1 and 7.1 · med (est)
