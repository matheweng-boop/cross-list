4. Pain points and numbers (our reading)

  • 4.1 · Inrush example: 500 µF at 800 V stores ½·500 µF·(800 V)² = 160 J; 800 V / 50 mΩ ≈ 16 kA first peak; with 50 Ω: 16 A peak, R·C = 25 ms, about 75 ms to 95 %; the resistor absorbs about the stored energy (160 J) per pre-charge · low (est; capacitance and loop resistance are typical assumptions, not from a source)
  • 4.2 · DC arcs (no current zero), contactor blow-out and gas-filled chambers, limited breaks under load, welding after inrush or short; HVIL with first-breaking pins · low (common engineering knowledge; not researched in this run)
  • 4.3 · Solid-state vs contactor (losses, microsecond fault turn-off, no galvanic gap, need for an isolated driver and sensing) · low (est)
  • 4.4 · 400 V class about 250–450 V, 800 V class about 550–900 V; fast chargers to 1000 V · low (common figures; not researched in this run)
