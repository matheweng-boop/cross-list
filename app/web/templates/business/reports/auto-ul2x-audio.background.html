5. Our estimates (est)

  • Cars with rear screens or a passenger gaming feature: about 6–10 M a year by 2030 (6–10 % of production), mostly China MPVs and SUVs and US three-row SUVs and minivans; no public unit count found.
  • Share of those with OEM-supplied wireless headphones or a zone unit: 30–50 %; UL2X share of those sockets: 10–30 %; so 0.2–1.5 M hubs a year.
  • Karaoke kits 0.5–1 M a year, mostly China: no public count; several brands and Tesla sell them.
  • Content: automotive arbiter $3.5–6, consumer receiver $2–3.2, client $1.5–2.8, hub FEM $0.6–1.0; about $8 a car with two clients. Revenue about $5–15 M a year by 2030.
  • Bluetooth Classic 100–200 ms: typical codec and buffer values, not measured in a car.
  • Accessories (headphones, microphones, USB receivers) need no automotive grade; the hub inside the rear-seat unit or head unit does.
  • Qualification 12–24 months if the die holds at −40 to 85 °C (earlier cases quote 12–36 months from trade press).
  • Ranking of the use cases: fit × volume × evidence, our judgement.
