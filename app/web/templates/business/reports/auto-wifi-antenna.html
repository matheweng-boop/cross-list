Wi-Fi 6, 6E and 7 in cars, Wi-Fi and antenna synergies, and the gaps we could fill

In brief

  1. Wi-Fi is now in most new cars, and 6 GHz is coming. About 99 M automotive Wi-Fi devices ship in 2026, 21 % of them 6 GHz-capable (IDC, quoted by the Wi-Fi Alliance). The first Wi-Fi 7 car programme starts production in 2027 (LG Innotek, Qualcomm chip). Wi-Fi sits in the head unit, the telematics unit (TCU), rear-seat screens and, more and more, in the roof antenna module next to V2X.
  2. We cover the antenna side well, the Wi-Fi amplifier side poorly. With Qorvo we have automotive (AEC-Q) switches, V2X front ends, GNSS, cellular and BAW coexistence filters. But only 3 of our 89 Wi-Fi FEMs are AEC-Q, none of them a Wi-Fi 6 or 7 power amplifier at 5 or 6 GHz, and the three automotive Wi-Fi filters are still Preview.
  3. Synergy: Skyworks + Qorvo together cover almost every block of a roof or smart-antenna module (cellular, GNSS, V2X, Wi-Fi switch and LNA, UWB, filters, switches). The missing blocks are the automotive 5/6 GHz Wi-Fi power amplifier, an automotive 5/6 GHz splitter for Wi-Fi 7 multi-link, and an automotive antenna tuner.
  4. Five gaps, strongest first: (1) an automotive Wi-Fi 7 FEM at 6 GHz and 5 GHz, AEC-Q100 Grade 2; (2) the 5–7 GHz coexistence filters out of Preview, with a sharper V2X edge; (3) an automotive 5/6 GHz multiplexer for multi-link (MLO) on one antenna; (4) UWB channel 5 and 6 GHz Wi-Fi share the same spectrum in the cabin; (5) an automotive antenna tuner (weak evidence). Gap 1 and 2 are the enabling step of the business case.
  5. Next wave: Wi-Fi 8 (802.11bn, final approval expected May 2028), Wi-Fi sensing (802.11bf, published 2025: child presence detection with the car's own Wi-Fi) and Wi-Fi positioning (802.11bk). Cars follow phones and routers by about 3–4 years (est).

The roof or smart-antenna module is where these pairs come together. GM says it moves the TCU, radios and antennas into one roof “Connectivity Hub Module” with an Ethernet link; LG has a 5G roof telematics unit in production (Aug 2026); Harman and Continental sell roof units that list Wi-Fi. For the decision: We already sell almost every block of a roof antenna module with Qorvo; the two blocks that would make Wi-Fi part of the set are an automotive 5/6 GHz Wi-Fi FEM and the coexistence filters in production.

2. Gaps and new technology

Each gap: what is missing, why it matters, the public evidence and how often the function shows up in the 4,186 public reference designs in the site's data (TI, Renesas, ROHM, Murata). Those designs show the Wi-Fi radio (156 designs, 13 automotive) but never its RF front end: Wi-Fi FEMs, filters and tuners appear in 0 of them, because they sit inside modules. Public reference designs are therefore no guide to demand here.

  1. Automotive Wi-Fi 7 FEM at 6 GHz and 5 GHz, AEC-Q100 Grade 2 (−40 to 105 °C). We have consumer Wi-Fi 7 FEMs (SKY85639-11, SKY85509-11, QPF4659, QPF4507) but no automotive Wi-Fi PA above 802.11ac; Qorvo's earlier automotive Wi-Fi FEMs went end of life in 2024. Qualcomm pairs its automotive Wi-Fi 7 chip with its own AEC-Q100 FEM (QXM19xx), and a public module comparison shows an external FEM gives about 21 dBm versus 13–16 dBm on chip. A 6 GHz part should aim at linearity and receive noise, not high power: in-car 6 GHz power is capped (US very low power; EU low-power indoor not allowed in road vehicles).

    gap · Found in 0 public reference designs (FEM hidden in modules) · evidence: u-blox RUBY-W2 and JODY-W6 product summaries; LG Innotek 2027 order · enabling step of the case (feature A5/A6).

  2. 5–7 GHz coexistence filters in production, with a sharper V2X edge. SKY5A1005 (V2X notch), SKY5A1006 (5 GHz) and SKY5A1008 (6 GHz) are defined but Preview. The notch needs about 40 MHz to go from pass to stop; the 6 GHz Wi-Fi band starts at 5.925 GHz, right where V2X ends, so a filter that passes all of 6 GHz Wi-Fi and still protects V2X needs a much steeper edge (est). US DOT measured in-car Wi-Fi cutting C-V2X range by more than half; NTIA and DOT asked for −37 dBm/MHz at the band edge.

    defined, Preview · Found in 0 public reference designs · evidence: NTIA/US DOT exhibit (2023); u-blox RUBY-W2 lists optional coexistence filters · sells even into designs with an on-chip PA.

  3. Automotive 5/6 GHz multiplexer for Wi-Fi 7 multi-link (MLO) on one antenna. Multi-link runs the 5 and 6 GHz radios at the same time; on a shared antenna the two need a splitter with high isolation, or one radio deafens the other. Our only 5/6 GHz part is the consumer SKY85929-11 meshplexer (Preview). Automotive Wi-Fi 7 modules already advertise 2×2 multi-link on 5 + 6 GHz.

    gap · Found in 0 public reference designs · evidence: u-blox RUBY-W2 (MLO on 5 + 6 GHz) · reuse: SKY85929-11 die and the automotive BAW flow (est).

  4. UWB and 6 GHz Wi-Fi in the same cabin. UWB channel 5 (6.24–6.74 GHz) lies inside the 6 GHz Wi-Fi band, and digital-key UWB anchors sit in the same doors, pillars and roof as Wi-Fi antennas. Today the radios take turns (time sharing); a filtered UWB receive path for channel 9 (about 7.7–8.2 GHz) that rejects 6 GHz Wi-Fi would let both run at once (est). We have the automotive UWB SoCs (QPF5100Q, DW3300Q) and a UWB switch + LNA (QM14068, not automotive), but no such filter.

    SoC yes, filter no · Found in 2 public reference designs (Murata UWB modules with a SAW/BAW filter; neither automotive) · evidence: IEEE 802.15.4 channel plan; see the UWB 802.15.4ab case (est on the need).

  5. Automotive antenna tuner (weak evidence). A shark fin must cover 617 MHz to 6 GHz in a few centimetres; published fin antennas reach only about 6 dB isolation at low band. Phones solve this with aperture tuners, and Qorvo makes tunable capacitors, but none is automotive and no public car design names a tuner. Worth one question to an antenna maker, not a product yet (est).

    gap, unproven need · Found in 0 public reference designs · evidence: IEEE fin-antenna letter (2024, search snippet); no carmaker statement found.

The integrated radio head per antenna (Wi-Fi 5 + V2X + Wi-Fi 6 GHz in one module) is the headline of the business case auto-wifi7-fem and is not repeated here.

For the decision: Gaps 1 and 2 reuse dies and filters we have and serve every car with Wi-Fi; gap 3 follows once Wi-Fi 7 multi-link reaches cars; gaps 4 and 5 need a customer to confirm the need first.

3. Where else we see opportunities

  • EV chargers and charging depots: Wi-Fi radios appear in 5 Renesas EV charger and wallbox reference designs; the charger talks to the car and the cloud. Industrial grade, not AEC-Q: our consumer Wi-Fi FEMs fit as they are.
  • Trucks, buses and fleets: roof antennas that combine 3× Wi-Fi 6E, V2X and an active GNSS amplifier are already sold for fleets (Pulse/Larsen, search snippet); passenger Wi-Fi in buses and trains needs the same filters.
  • Two-wheelers and agricultural machines: telematics boxes with Wi-Fi and GPS (Renesas 4G + GPS telematics box design).
  • Wi-Fi sensing in the cabin: child presence detection with two of the car's own Wi-Fi radios and software (Murata with Origin AI); little new hardware, but a reason to keep a Wi-Fi radio in the cabin even if the TCU moves to the roof (est).

For the decision: These are add-ons; none changes the first steps, but chargers and fleets can take consumer or industrial Wi-Fi parts now.

5. Market trend

  • Volume: about 99 M automotive Wi-Fi devices in 2026, 21 % 6 GHz-capable (IDC via Wi-Fi Alliance); 83 % of new cars connected in 2025 (Berg Insight, snippet).
  • Generations: Wi-Fi 5 is still the majority; Wi-Fi 6 cars grew 142 % in 2025, to about 11 % of Wi-Fi cars, led by China (Counterpoint, snippets). First Wi-Fi 7 automotive chip 2024 (Qualcomm), modules sampling 2025 (u-blox), first car production 2027 (LG Innotek). Wi-Fi 7 at 10–20 % of automotive Wi-Fi by 2030 (est, from the case).
  • Who supplies the front end: Qualcomm bundles its own automotive FEM; NXP (AW693) and Synaptics put the PA on the chip; module makers (u-blox, LG Innotek, Murata) choose. China uses 2.4 + 5 GHz with local FEM suppliers.
  • Architecture: radios and antennas move into the roof module (GM, LG, Harman, Continental); coexistence becomes a filter problem inside one box.
  • Value: automotive Wi-Fi modules about $3.7–3.9 B in 2025 (analyst releases, low confidence).

For the decision: Volume is there and moving to 6 GHz; the share open to an external FEM is the uncertain part, while filters fit every design.

Risks

  • Qualcomm's chip-plus-FEM bundle and on-chip PAs at NXP and Synaptics may leave few sockets for an external Wi-Fi FEM.
  • All our automotive Wi-Fi filters and two key front ends are Preview; customers ask 10–15 years of supply after Qorvo's 2024 end-of-life notices.
  • In-car 6 GHz power limits cap the value of a 6 GHz PA.
  • Automotive qualification and design-in take 12–36 months (trade press), so a 2027 Wi-Fi 7 start is already decided.
  • Qorvo data is incomplete where only qorvo.com lists the parts (not requested); QPF4550Q is known only from an evaluation board.

For the decision: Start with what sells into every design (the filters) and ask the Wi-Fi BU about an automotive die before committing to the FEM.
