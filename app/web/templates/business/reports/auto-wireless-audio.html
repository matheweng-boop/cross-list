Wireless audio in the car: what fits, what stays wired, and which of our parts to offer first

28 Oct 2026 · Preliminary report from the owner's request of 28 Oct 2026: “Can you do a preliminary research report on topic wireless audio in a car?” It builds on the new-market idea “Automotive rear-seat / multi-zone audio” in our evidence (low-latency audio chips for rear-seat headphones) and on the report Wi-Fi 6, 6E and 7 in cars. Built from BizDev data (Skyworks and Qorvo catalogues with the AEC-Q rule, the Systems library, 4,186 public reference designs) and public web sources. “est” = our estimate. Every figure and link: background notes.

In brief

  1. Latency decides what can go wireless. Road-noise cancellation needs the sensor signal in under 2 ms and the whole loop within about 4 ms; the wired A2B bus carries audio in under 50 µs. Bluetooth needs tens of milliseconds, our own UL2X link under 9 ms. So noise cancellation, safety chimes and the main speakers stay wired (est); passenger headphones, audio sharing and add-on speakers can go wireless.
  2. Bluetooth LE Audio is the link carmakers will use first. It brings the LC3 codec (better than SBC at half the bit rate), several synchronised streams and Auracast (one sender, any number of headphones). Automotive LE Audio chips exist (Infineon CYW89310, NXP AW300, Qualcomm-based u-blox RUBY-W2); Barrot sells Auracast modules to Chinese carmakers. We found no carmaker announcement of Auracast in a production car.
  3. Our car-side parts are automotive today: QPF4239Q 2.4 GHz FEM, SKYA21040 switch + LNA, QPQ2254Q 2.4 GHz BAW against LTE bands 40/41, SKY5A2108 and QPC8019Q antenna switches, Si5332-AM clocks and the ACT4751 12 V buck. They attach to any Bluetooth or Wi-Fi audio hub, whatever link wins.
  4. Our differentiator is consumer grade. SKY76360/76361 (UL2X, 2.4 GHz: up to 8 uncompressed channels, under 9 ms, clients in sync to 1 µs) and SKY76303 (5 GHz, 5 clients, under 20 ms) are built for exactly wireless speakers and headsets, but run at 0–70 °C. Headphones are accessories and can use them as they are; the car-side hub needs an automotive version.
  5. Gaps: a Bluetooth LE Audio chip, an automotive audio SoC, an audio codec, speaker-level class-D amplifiers and an automotive crystal oscillator. Answer: front ends first, then test demand for an automotive UL2X; no full audio system (est, low confidence).

Bluetooth latency figures are typical values for the link and codec (est); the real value depends on the stack and buffers. UWB for audio is real in consumer headsets, not in cars; in the car UWB is busy with keys and child presence (see UWB child presence).

Requirements

  • Latency: lip-sync with a screen needs tens of milliseconds (est); noise cancellation needs about 2 ms transport (Molex, snippet). Only wired buses meet the second.
  • Quality and codec: LC3 for Bluetooth; uncompressed 24-bit for UL2X and SKY76303; A2B carries uncompressed audio.
  • Coexistence: 2.4 GHz carries Bluetooth audio, Bluetooth keys, Wi-Fi 2.4 and the phone's own links, next to LTE bands 40/41; a BAW filter (QPQ2254Q) and good receive linearity help. UWB sits at 6.5–8 GHz and does not crowd 2.4 GHz, but channel 5 overlaps 6 GHz Wi-Fi.
  • Power: headphones run on batteries; our UL2X client is “ultra-low-power”, and LC3 saves bit rate. Our chargers (ACT2861) are not automotive, which is fine inside an accessory.
  • Functional safety: chimes and warnings must sound even if a radio fails; we keep them wired (est). Not enough data on an ASIL for the chime path.
  • Security and pairing: LE Audio links are encrypted and an Auracast broadcast can carry a code, so a car can keep its stream to its own passengers (est).

For the decision: On the car side we fill the front end, filter, switch, clock and power blocks with automotive parts today; our real audio differentiator, the UL2X link, is consumer grade and only fits the headphone side as it is.

2. Gaps and new technology

  1. An automotive low-latency audio link (UL2X, AEC-Q100 Grade 3). SKY76360/76361 already give what wireless rear speakers and multi-headphone systems need: fixed latency under 9 ms, up to 8 uncompressed channels and clients in sync to 1 µs, with an ultra-low-latency data link (about 1 ms) that also fits game controllers in the cabin. Bluetooth cannot match the sync and latency; WiSA and UWB audio are not in cars. But it is proprietary: a passenger's own earbuds will not receive it.

    consumer part exists · evidence: Skyworks SKY76360/76361 and SKY76303 product summaries; no car programme found · reuse: same dies, qualification and an automotive package (est).

  2. An automotive Bluetooth LE Audio front end, and later 5/6 GHz Bluetooth. Combo chips have the PA and LNA on board (Infineon CYW89310: “integrated on-chip PAs, LNAs”), so an external FEM sells only for range, coexistence or several antennas. The Bluetooth SIG is defining LE operation in the 5 and 6 GHz bands and a higher-rate PHY up to about 8 Mbps; that would bring Bluetooth audio onto the same 5/6 GHz front ends as Wi-Fi, where our automotive PA is a gap (Wi-Fi report, gap 1).

    2.4 GHz yes, 5/6 GHz no PA · evidence: Bluetooth 2025 Market Update, technology roadmap; Infineon CYW89310 page.

  3. A Bluetooth LE Audio / Auracast chip. We have none; Qualcomm, NXP, Infineon, Realtek and Barrot do. Building one is a new SoC in a crowded market (est): not recommended.

    gap, not recommended · evidence: NXP AW300 and Infineon CYW89xxx listings (snippets and page).

  4. Speaker-level automotive class-D amplifier and audio codec. Needed for any wireless speaker node, and already a gap in the car radio report. TI, NXP, ST and ADI sell them with their buses.

    gap · 16 automotive audio reference designs, all with other vendors' amplifiers or codecs.

  5. Audio-rate references from the automotive clock tree. Zones and wireless speakers play in sync only from one shared clock; Si5332-AM is automotive, but audio-rate outputs (24.576 MHz family) are not in our data. A question for the automotive clock tree case.

    Not enough data · our estimate of the need.

For the decision: Gap 1 is the only one where we would bring something new to the car; gaps 2 and 5 are add-ons to parts we have; gaps 3 and 4 are better left to partners.

3. Where else we see opportunities

  • Buses, coaches and trains: Auracast lets every passenger listen to the guide or the film on their own earbuds; the hub is a fixed Bluetooth transmitter with a front end and filters (est). The SIG expects 2.5 million public Auracast locations by 2030.
  • Motorcycles and helmets: named by Barrot as a next Auracast field (“smart riding”); consumer or industrial grade, so today's Bluetooth FEMs and the UL2X client fit as they are (est).
  • In-cabin gaming: the UL2X data link of about 1 ms suits game controllers for screens in parked EVs (est; no carmaker evidence found).
  • Musician gear, conference and venue systems: covered in our new-market evidence; the owner rated them low volume (26 Oct 2026).

For the decision: These are consumer or industrial sockets for parts we already sell; none needs an automotive part first.

4. Market trend and who is doing it

  • Volume: Bluetooth in about 87 % of new cars, 109 M vehicle units forecast for 2024 (Bluetooth SIG / ABI, snippet, low). About 99 M automotive Wi-Fi devices in 2026 (IDC via Wi-Fi Alliance, med, from the Wi-Fi report). 3 billion LE Audio devices a year by 2028, nearly all new phones (Bluetooth SIG 2024 forecast, med).
  • Value: automotive Bluetooth market estimates disagree eightfold (about $1.3 B to $11.2 B for 2025, analyst snippets), so none is used. Rear-seat wireless headphones and wireless car speakers: Not Enough Data.
  • Chip vendors: Qualcomm (automotive Wi-Fi 7 QCA6797AQ; the u-blox RUBY-W2 module on it lists LE Audio and Bluetooth 5.4), NXP (AW300 Bluetooth with LE Audio and Auracast, AEC-Q100 Grade 2/3; AW693 and 88Q9098 Wi-Fi + Bluetooth, snippets), Infineon (CYW89310 Bluetooth 5.4/6.0 with LE Audio unicast and Auracast, −40 to 85 °C; CYW89570/89550/89530 Wi-Fi 6/6E + Bluetooth; CYW89829 named for LE Audio rear-seat entertainment), Barrot (Auracast modules for Chinese carmakers), Realtek and Nordic (no automotive LE Audio part found). ADI owns the wired A2B bus.
  • Carmakers and Tier-1s: Bluetooth headphones for rear-seat systems at Ford and Lincoln (accessory pages); Rivian's Bluetooth Camp Speaker; Bose headrest speakers in production; Harman, Bose and ADI work on sound zones. No Auracast production car found (search, 28 Oct 2026).
  • Trend: from infrared to Bluetooth headphones; LE Audio follows the phones; Bluetooth moves to 5/6 GHz and higher rates; wired A2B and Ethernet keep noise cancellation, microphones and amplifiers.

For the decision: Volume is large but sits inside combo chips with the amplifier on board; the open sockets for us are the front end around them and, if a carmaker asks for it, a low-latency link the combo chips cannot match.

Risks

  • Carmakers may stay with Bluetooth only: phones and earbuds speak Bluetooth, and a proprietary link needs our receiver in every headphone.
  • On-chip PAs and LNAs in combo chips leave few sockets for an external 2.4 GHz FEM.
  • Our audio SoCs are 0–70 °C; automotive qualification takes 12–36 months (trade press) and may need a redesign.
  • Demand evidence is thin: no Auracast car, no wireless main speakers in production, no unit counts for rear-seat headphones.
  • Qorvo data is incomplete where only qorvo.com lists the parts (not requested); molex.com and sparkmicro.com could not be read, so their figures are snippets.

For the decision: Start with the automotive front ends that attach anyway, and spend on an automotive audio link only after a Tier-1 asks for it.
