Standalone filters, 1–11 GHz: background notes

Correction, 20 Oct 2026: these notes treat Trans-Tech and Lark as Skyworks businesses. Trans-Tech was sold to Pacific Industrial Development (PIDC) in April 2023 and bought Lark in 2025; both are now competitors (confirmed by the BizDev owner). Read “Trans-Tech / Lark” routes below as partner or acquisition options.

Prepared 20 Oct 2026 from BizDev data (Skyworks / Qorvo catalogues, 3,990 collected competitor RF parts, 219 application block diagrams with the band each RF block needs) and public web sources. Figures marked “est” are estimates; “unverified” rests on a single or secondary source.

In brief

  1. Wide bandwidth above ~25 % is not acoustic territory. BAW reaches ~7 % coupling (AlN), ~18 % with scandium, XBAR ~25 %. Full n77 (24 %), UWB channels 5–9 (28 %), X-band (40 %) and 2–18 GHz need LTCC, thin-film / IPD, ceramic, cavity or switched / tunable filters.19 21 23 24
  2. Skyworks' own standalone line is narrow and acoustic: 10 BAW filters for Wi-Fi 5 / 6 GHz and V2X. With Qorvo (combination completed Oct 20261 2) it adds 175 BAW / SAW / ceramic filters, but about half are obsolete or NRND and none is active above 8 GHz.
  3. Competitors fill 4–11 GHz with non-acoustic catalogues: Mini-Circuits alone lists ~110 active filters there (LTCC, cavity, thin film), including multi-GHz passbands such as 3.1–7.1 GHz.
  4. Best near-term bets are acoustic and adjacent: Wi-Fi 7/8 coexistence, automotive coexistence, 5G FR1 sub-band and hybrid n77 filters. Wideband 1–11 GHz needs a non-acoustic capability, where Trans-Tech (ceramic) and Lark (custom to 40 GHz), both Skyworks-owned, are the starting point.13 14 17

  2. Automotive coexistence: Wi-Fi 5/6 GHz, C-V2X band 47, GNSS L1/L5. SKY5A1005/6/8 and Qorvo QPQ2200Q already exist; AEC-Q qualification keeps newcomers out and the Akoustis C-V2X part is gone.

  4. Radio-altimeter and C-band earth-station protection, 3.98–4.4 GHz. US airline fleets must be retrofitted by the end of 2030, funded from the auction (single trade-press source); earth-station LNBs need sharper edges. High power and AS9100 suit Trans-Tech ceramic and Lark cavity filters.

  5. UWB channel 5/9 filters. ~1.4 B UWB devices by 2030 (ABI). Today's parts are LTCC (Abracon, Johanson) at a few dollars; Skyworks has no UWB filter and no LTCC line, so this needs build, buy or partner.

    Fit: capability gap · High volume, low price.45 46 66

    Fit: partly · BizDev demand: 6 applications each at X and Ku band.5 17 33 49 55 69

Why acoustic filters run out of width

  • An acoustic filter's width is capped by its resonators' electromechanical coupling (k²): about 7 % for AlN BAW, up to ~18 % with scandium-doped AlN, about 25 % and more for XBAR in thin lithium niobate.21 23 24
  • A plain ladder filter passes well below k² (est). Wider parts add passive matching: our widest active filters are ~18–20 % (SKY85921-11 Wi-Fi 6 GHz; QPB1033 bank). Full n77 (24 %) has been shown with an IPD + acoustic hybrid at about 2.5 dB loss.19 26
  • Above ~25 %, or over octaves, the choices are LC / LTCC, thin film / IPD, ceramic, cavity, tunable filters or switched filter banks.

3. Combined-product ideas

1. Wi-Fi 7/8 BAW multiplexer and coexistence kit (2.4/5/6 GHz)

  • What it is: grow the SKY85929/31/32 triplexers/diplexers into a family, sold with SKY85922/23-11, QPQ5604 and QPQ5610 standalone BAW, and paired with our Wi-Fi FEMs as a reference design [A6][S27][S29].
  • Competitors:
    • Murata LFD182G45PJEF833 (LTCC 2.4 / 5–7.125 GHz diplexer)
    • Johanson 2450DP14A6000001T and similar (0603 tri-band combo diplexer)
    • Yageo DPX1608LKE5R2460A [A7]
    • Qualcomm HBS diplexer / QXF8xxx (BizDev data: blocks/research/rf_platform_docs.yaml)
    • Murata XBAR [S30]
  • Why we'd win: ours is the only catalogue BAW-based multiplexer we found. BAW skirts allow 5/6 GHz split operation where LTCC cannot. Broadcom keeps FBAR inside its FEMs [S83], and Akoustis has exited [S8].
  • Why we might not: LTCC diplexers are a fraction of the price (est). Wi-Fi 8 may pull the filter into FEMs, ours or competitors'.

2. Filtered switch-LNA receive module for 5G small cells, FWA and upper C-band

  • What it is: QPQ3509 / QPQ3550 / QPQ4900 BAW plus QPB9850 or QPB9362 switch-LNA (or SKY67210-11) in one LGA. A second version would use a BAW + IPD hybrid for full n77.
  • Our base: Qorvo already launched QPQ3509 and QPB9850 together as a two-chip set [A2]. Skyworks already integrates the same function for handsets (SKY53759-11, SKY5A5009) [A8].
  • Competitors:
    • ADI ADRF5545A (dual switch + LNA, 2.4–4.2 GHz, no filter) [A11]
    • LTCC filters: Johanson n77/n79 parts, Mini-Circuits BFCN-3700+ [S41][S43]
  • Why we'd win: one-footprint filter+switch+LNA with BAW rejection; no competitor product found (unverified). The upper C-band auction (2027) and n77/n79 sub-band radios add sockets [S71].
  • Why we might not: OEMs often prefer separate parts so they can change the band. Full n77 still needs the hybrid. QPQ4900 lead times of 98–112 weeks hurt [S82].

3. Defense S/C-band protected switched-filter-bank receive module

  • What it is: extend QPB1029/1033/1034/1036 [A1] into C-band using QPQ BAW at 3.5–6 GHz, and add a front limiter (TGL2927-SM, TGL2206) and LNA (CMD283C3, CMD270P3).
  • Our base: high-power ceramic only through a partner (Trans-Tech was sold in 2023); Qorvo's DoD Trusted 1A BAW fab [S5].
  • Competitors:
    • MACOM MAFL-011202 (2–18 GHz, 3-band with bypass, about 6 dB IL, 9×7 mm) [A9]
    • Mercury AM3163 [S58]
    • ED2 FB010BW16SG [A9]
    • Quantic PMI 8SFB-2G18G-SFF [A9]
    • ADI ADMV8818 / ADMV8913 tunable [A4][A5]
  • Why we'd win: BAW rejection in 6×6 mm, plus Trusted fab and our own limiters and LNAs.
  • Why we might not: BAW channels are narrow (≤ about 20 % each), so octave 2–18 GHz coverage needs Lark thin-film/cavity or GaAs MMIC filters we don't catalogue. Tunable MMICs are displacing banks in SWaP-limited EW [A4].

4. X-band limiter + filter + LNA (or a filter added to the X-band FEMs)

  • What it is: QPP2209 / TGL2209-SM limiter, then a Lark thin-film or new GaAs MMIC 8–12 GHz BPF, then CMD319C3 / QPL1000 / QPM6000. The alternative is to fold the filter into QPF5002 / QPF5012 / QPM5811.
  • Competitors:
    • Mercury AM3235 MMIC BPF [S34]
    • Smiths Planar X [S33]
    • Knowles B096QC2S [S73]
    • ADI ADMV8913 (7–12 GHz tunable HPF/LPF, about 5 dB IL) [A5]
    • MACOM limiters (BizDev data: catalog/rf_competitors/macom.yaml)
  • Why we'd win: the complete X-band chain from one Trusted supplier.
  • Why we might not: no catalogue X-band filter today (Lark custom only). It is a crowded, entrenched niche with low volume.

5. UWB filtered LNA module for channels 5 and 9, sold with Qorvo UWB SoCs

  • What it is: QM14068 (switch + LNA 6.2–8.3 GHz, 1×1.2 mm, made for QM33/DW3000) [A12] plus a channel filter, sold as a reference set with Qorvo UWB SoCs (14 uwb_soc entries, BizDev data: catalog/qorvo_parts.yaml).
  • Filter options: per-channel BAW is plausible, since ch5 is about 8 % and ch9 about 6 % fractional bandwidth (est). A both-channel filter (28 %) needs LTCC or IPD [S45][S46].
  • Competitors: Abracon AFII-LC and Johanson Ch9 BPF (filters only) [S45][S46]. No UWB filter + LNA module from Infineon, NXP or Murata turned up (unverified).
  • Why we'd win: captive pull from our own SoC, and about 1.4 B UWB devices by 2030 [S66].
  • Why we might not: LTCC filters cost cents to a few dollars (est), and we have no LTCC.

6. GNSS / L-band / NTN filtered LNA family (L1/L5, L-band NTN n253–n256)

  • What it is: build on SKY55951-11, SKY65943-11 and SKY5A2110 with Qorvo SAW QPQ1060/61/62/65 and the QPQ1063 diplexer. Add a high-power defense variant with a partner ceramic diplexer (e.g. Trans-Tech TT49778E).
  • Competitors: Taoglas TFM.100B / TFM.115A (SAW-LNA-SAW-LNA, 20×18 mm) [A10]; Infineon BGA123L4 (LNA only) (BizDev data: catalog/rf_competitors/infineon.yaml).
  • Why we'd win: we own both acoustic filters and LNAs, AEC-Q automotive grade, and 2×1.6 mm size vs 20×18 mm modules [A10]. NTN bands are a new socket (BizDev data: business/evidence/new_markets.yaml baw_filters).
  • Why we might not: below 2 GHz this is SAW and LNA commodity, with low price per unit.

7. (Lower priority) High-power ceramic/cavity filter + limiter for radio-altimeter and C-band earth-station protection

  • What it is: a partner ceramic or cavity filter (e.g. Trans-Tech, Lark) plus Skyworks CLA460x limiter diodes [S14][S17].
  • Competitors: K&L, Knowles, Smiths [S49][S52][S33]; filtered LNBs [S70].
  • Why it is lower priority: the timing (retrofit by 2030) and the AS9100 fit help [S72], but volume is small.

Risks

  • Market shape: BAW is ~91 % Broadcom + Qorvo by revenue, and most filter value sits inside modules; the standalone 1–11 GHz market is likely in the low billions (est).80
  • Pricing: commodity LTCC sells for about $4; Chinese suppliers dominate massive-MIMO ceramic filters.43 47
  • Capacity and integration: Qorvo's SAW production is being consolidated, and post-merger cost targets could prune low-volume standalone SKUs (est).5
  • IP: XBAR belongs to Murata/Resonant; Akoustis' IP went to a SpaceX subsidiary; Qorvo has enforced BAW IP in court.6 8 30
  • Data: filter market figures differ 2–3× between firms and are mostly paywalled summaries ($10–16 B total RF filters in 2024).36 78 79
