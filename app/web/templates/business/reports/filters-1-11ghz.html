In brief

  1. The best bets combine a filter with parts we already sell. For a filtered receive chain we have every other block from 1 to 11 GHz: LNAs, switches, limiters, PAs and multiplexers. Only our filters stop at about 7 GHz.
  2. Our standalone filters are narrowband acoustic (BAW/SAW). Wide bandwidth (over ~25 %, e.g. full n77, UWB ch5–9, X-band) needs ceramic, LTCC, IPD or cavity filters. We have none of these since Trans-Tech (with Lark) was sold in 2023; it is now a competitor, so wideband means build, buy or partner.
  3. Wideband need is real and growing: Wi-Fi 6E/7 (1.2 GHz of spectrum), full n77, UWB, satellite terminals, anti-jam GNSS, SDR, test and defense EW all need passbands over ~25 % or filters switched over octaves. Our best entry is a switched filter bank: our switches and BAW around partner LTCC or thin-film filters (est).
  4. Top ideas: a Wi-Fi 7/8 BAW multiplexer family, a filtered switch-LNA receive module for 5G small cells, a protected switched filter bank for defense, and a wideband switched preselector for SDR and test.

2. Wideband filters: where they are needed and how we could cover them

“Wideband” here means a passband wider than about 25 % of its centre frequency, or one that must be moved or switched over an octave or more. Acoustic filters (our BAW and SAW) cannot reach that alone; the need is real and growing.

Who makes them

Competitors, summary level: Mini-Circuits (in BizDev data ~330 band-pass filters centred at 1–11 GHz, 52 of them wider than 25 %, mostly LTCC, lumped and thin film), Knowles (thin-film, ceramic and cavity filters and filter banks for defense), Murata (LTCC filters and diplexers; XBAR), Anatech Electronics (cavity, ceramic and LC, custom), Trans-Tech with Lark (ceramic, cavity, custom to 40 GHz), Johanson, Yageo and Kyocera AVX (LTCC, thin film), ADI (tunable MMIC filters to 18 GHz), and MACOM, Mercury, Quantic PMI and ED2 (switched filter banks).

  • have BAW and SAW up to ~20 % width and ~7 GHz; four switched filter banks plus QPB1055 (C-band); ~131 active switches (QPC2180 SP8T 0.1–8 GHz, QPC6144 SP4T to 10 GHz); limiters and LNAs to 20 GHz (QPM1000); varactors and PIN diodes.
  • gap No LTCC, ceramic-resonator, cavity, thin-film or IPD filter in either catalogue (“ceramic” appears only as a package). Trans-Tech (ceramic) and Lark (cavity) were sold and now compete; no tunable filter; nothing above ~8 GHz.

For the decision: The wideband need is real (Wi-Fi 6E/7, full n77, UWB, defense, test, SDR), but we can only meet it by combining our switches and BAW with partner LTCC or thin-film filters; the switched bank is the fastest entry (est).

3. Where else we see opportunities

  • Automotive coexistence (Wi-Fi, C-V2X, GNSS): parts exist (SKY5A100x, QPQ2200Q); AEC-Q keeps newcomers out.
  • Radio-altimeter and C-band earth-station protection (3.98–4.4 GHz): US retrofit by 2030; today a ceramic and cavity niche (Trans-Tech, Lark, K&L), so only via a partner.
  • 6G FR3 (7–8 GHz) and mmWave IF filters: BAW for FR3 already sampling; timing ~2029+ (est).
  • X-band chain (limiter + filter + LNA): we have every block except an X-band filter; crowded, low volume.
  • BizDev demand: RF filter blocks are needed in 25 S-band, 22 C-band and 6 each X- and Ku-band applications. At X and Ku, only competitors serve them today.

For the decision: These are second-wave markets; none changes the first pick, and like the wideband filters, C-band protection and X-band would need a partner.

4. Market trend

  • Filters are moving into modules. Most filter value sits inside FEMs; BAW is ~91 % Broadcom + Qorvo. Standalone 1–11 GHz is likely low billions (est).
  • New bands drive new sockets: Wi-Fi 7/8 (~118 M APs in 2026, unverified), US upper C-band auction (2027), UWB (~1.4 B devices by 2030), FR3.
  • Wideband catalogue filters are commodity. LTCC sells at ~$4 or less; Mini-Circuits lists ~110 active filters at 4–11 GHz.
  • Fewer acoustic rivals: Akoustis exited in 2025; XBAR (Murata) is the main new wideband acoustic technology.
  • Wider channels everywhere: 320 MHz Wi-Fi 7 channels, full-band 5G radios, LEO user terminals and direct-RF-sampling receivers all push filters wider; defense and test move from switched banks to tunable MMIC filters where size matters.

For the decision: Filter value is moving into modules and wideband catalogue filters are commodity, which supports combined products and a filter partner over a new LTCC line of our own (est).

Risks

  • Post-merger cost cuts could prune low-volume standalone parts; Qorvo's SAW production is being consolidated.
  • Our former ceramics business (Trans-Tech, with Lark) now competes in wideband, high-power and defense filters.
  • Filter market figures differ 2–3× between sources.
  • Qorvo data is still incomplete where only qorvo.com lists parts (blocked to automated access); 229 parts were added from public sources, mostly legacy.

For the decision: Before proposing anything, check that the post-merger plan keeps low-volume standalone filters and that a wideband filter partner would supply parts for our modules.
