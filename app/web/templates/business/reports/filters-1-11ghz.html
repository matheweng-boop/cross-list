Standalone filter opportunities, 1–11 GHz

Prepared 20 Oct 2026 from BizDev data (Skyworks / Qorvo catalogues, 3,990 collected competitor RF parts, 219 application block diagrams with the band each RF block needs) and public web sources. Figures marked “est” are estimates; “unverified” rests on a single or secondary source.

In brief

  1. Wide bandwidth above ~25 % is not acoustic territory. BAW reaches ~7 % coupling (AlN), ~18 % with scandium, XBAR ~25 %. Full n77 (24 %), UWB channels 5–9 (28 %), X-band (40 %) and 2–18 GHz need LTCC, thin-film / IPD, ceramic, cavity or switched / tunable filters.19 21 23 24
  2. Skyworks' own standalone line is narrow and acoustic: 10 BAW filters for Wi-Fi 5 / 6 GHz and V2X. With Qorvo (combination completed Oct 20261 2) it adds 175 BAW / SAW / ceramic filters, but about half are obsolete or NRND and none is active above 8 GHz.
  3. Competitors fill 4–11 GHz with non-acoustic catalogues: Mini-Circuits alone lists ~110 active filters there (LTCC, cavity, thin film), including multi-GHz passbands such as 3.1–7.1 GHz.
  4. Best near-term bets are acoustic and adjacent: Wi-Fi 7/8 coexistence, automotive coexistence, 5G FR1 sub-band and hybrid n77 filters. Wideband 1–11 GHz needs a non-acoustic capability, where Trans-Tech (ceramic) and Lark (custom to 40 GHz), both Skyworks-owned, are the starting point.13 14 17

Where BizDev's application diagrams need a filter

Applications with an RF filter block, by the band that block needs: S 2–4 GHz: 25 (5G massive MIMO, small cells, Wi-Fi 7 APs, CPE, telematics) · C 4–8 GHz: 22 (Wi-Fi 6E/7, 5G n77/n79, enterprise APs) · L 1–2 GHz: 12 · below 1 GHz: 12 · X and Ku: 6 each (radar, EW, satellite payload, backhaul, radar warning receiver) · Ka: 3. On the RF coverage page X- and Ku-band filters are “competitors only” gaps: needed by 6 applications each, served by Mini-Circuits (65 and 48 parts) and MACOM, with no active part of ours.

Opportunities, strongest first

  1. Wi-Fi 7/8 5 + 6 GHz coexistence BAW, sold standalone. Simultaneous 5 and 6 GHz operation needs ≥ 50 dB rejection across a 50–250 MHz guard. Skyworks SKY8592x plus Qorvo QPQ5604 / QPQ55xx now sit in one catalogue; Akoustis, which held tier-1 Wi-Fi 7 sockets, exited in 2025; Broadcom mostly sells its FBAR inside FEMs.

    Fit: have it · Demand: ~118 M Wi-Fi 7 APs forecast for 2026 (ABI, unverified); 22 BizDev applications need C-band filters.8 10 11 27 29 62 65 83

  2. Automotive coexistence: Wi-Fi 5/6 GHz, C-V2X band 47, GNSS L1/L5. SKY5A1005/6/8 and Qorvo QPQ2200Q already exist; AEC-Q qualification keeps newcomers out and the Akoustis C-V2X part is gone.

    Fit: have it · Value: automotive volume at good margins.28 67 68 86

  3. 5G FR1 infrastructure and CPE: n77 / n78 / n79 and upper C-band. Qorvo QPQ3509 (C-band), QPQ4900 (n79) and QPQ1298 (n41) are the base. The gap is a full-n77 standalone filter, which needs a BAW + IPD/LTCC hybrid (shown at 2.5 dB loss in IEEE work). The US upper C-band auction (3.98–4.14 GHz, 2027) adds new radios and edge filters.

    Fit: partly; needs a hybrid · Price point: QPQ4900 ~$4.5 at volume, 98–112-week reel lead time listed.19 26 40 41 61 71 82

  4. Radio-altimeter and C-band earth-station protection, 3.98–4.4 GHz. US airline fleets must be retrofitted by the end of 2030, funded from the auction (single trade-press source); earth-station LNBs need sharper edges. High power and AS9100 suit Trans-Tech ceramic and Lark cavity filters.

    Fit: via Trans-Tech / Lark · Timing: 2027–2030.14 17 70 71 72

  5. UWB channel 5/9 filters. ~1.4 B UWB devices by 2030 (ABI). Today's parts are LTCC (Abracon, Johanson) at a few dollars; Skyworks has no UWB filter and no LTCC line, so this needs build, buy or partner.

    Fit: capability gap · High volume, low price.45 46 66

  6. Defense: IFF / GNSS BAW, ceramic L/S/C, X-band thin film, 2–18 GHz banks. Qorvo's ITAR-free IFF BAW is ~10× smaller than ceramic; its Richardson BAW fab is DoD Trusted 1A; Lark covers custom work to 40 GHz. Sticky, high-value sockets, but Knowles, Smiths, Mercury, K&L and Spectrum Control are entrenched.

    Fit: partly · BizDev demand: 6 applications each at X and Ku band.5 17 33 49 55 69

  7. 6G FR3 (7–8 GHz) and mmWave IF (~3.3–7.1 GHz) filters. Skyworks already samples FR3 front ends and says it has BAW for early FR3; FR2 converters use 3.3–7.125 GHz IFs suited to BAW or LTCC.

    Fit: BAW in development · Timing tied to 6G (~2029+, est).74 75 76

What would be needed for true wideband 1–11 GHz

Skyworks' acoustic fabs cannot reach 25 %+ bandwidths or octave coverage. Three routes, in order of effort:

RouteCoversStarting pointTrade-off
Scale Trans-Tech / Lark into a catalogue lineL/S/C ceramic, custom cavity and lumped to 40 GHz~120-person AS9100 business, already ownedLow volume; custom engineering per program
Hybrid acoustic + IPD/LTCCfull n77, wider 5/6 GHz, FR3Skyworks + Qorvo BAW fabsNeeds IPD or LTCC process or a partner; best fit with existing customers
Buy or partner for LTCC / thin film / IPDUWB, X-band, multi-GHz catalogue filtersLTCC houses, glass-IPD firms (est)Commodity pricing at the low end; competes with Mini-Circuits, Murata, Kyocera AVX

Risks

  • Market shape: BAW is ~91 % Broadcom + Qorvo by revenue, and most filter value sits inside modules; the standalone 1–11 GHz market is likely in the low billions (est).80
  • Pricing: commodity LTCC sells for about $4; Chinese suppliers dominate massive-MIMO ceramic filters.43 47
  • Capacity and integration: Qorvo's SAW fab transfer is under way, the Singapore filter site must move by 2030, and post-merger cost targets could prune low-volume standalone SKUs (est).5 91
  • IP: XBAR belongs to Murata/Resonant; Akoustis' IP went to a SpaceX subsidiary; Qorvo has enforced BAW IP in court.6 8 30
  • Data: filter market figures differ 2–3× between firms and are mostly paywalled summaries ($10–16 B total RF filters in 2024).36 78 79
