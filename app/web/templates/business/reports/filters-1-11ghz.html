Standalone filter opportunities, 1–11 GHz

20 Oct 2026 · BizDev catalogue (Skyworks + Qorvo, competitors, 219 application diagrams) and public web sources · “est” = estimate. Data tables, detail and all sources are in the background notes.

In brief

  1. The best bets combine a filter with parts we already sell. For a filtered receive chain we have every other block from 1 to 11 GHz: LNAs, switches, limiters, PAs and multiplexers. Only our filters stop at about 7 GHz.
  2. Our standalone filters are narrowband acoustic (BAW/SAW). Wide bandwidth (over ~25 %, e.g. full n77, UWB ch5–9, X-band) needs ceramic, LTCC, IPD or cavity filters. Trans-Tech and Lark are our starting point there.
  3. Top three ideas: a Wi-Fi 7/8 BAW multiplexer family, a filtered switch-LNA receive module for 5G small cells, and a protected switched filter bank for defense.

Combined-product ideas, strongest first

  1. Wi-Fi 7/8 BAW multiplexer and coexistence kit. Grow SKY85929/31/32 into a family, sold with our 5/6 GHz BAW filters and Wi-Fi FEMs.

    have it · vs LTCC diplexers (Murata, Johanson, Yageo): we win on 5/6 GHz rejection, they win on price.

  2. Filtered switch-LNA module for 5G small cells, FWA and upper C-band. QPQ3509/QPQ4900 BAW plus QPB9850 switch-LNA in one package. A BAW + IPD hybrid version would cover full n77.

    have the parts · nearest competitor (ADI ADRF5545A) has no filter; no integrated equivalent found (unverified).

  3. Protected switched filter bank for defense, S/C-band. Extend QPB1033 into C-band and add our own limiter and LNA, built in the Trusted BAW fab.

    partly · vs MACOM, Mercury, ADI tunable filters; octave coverage needs Lark or MMIC filters.

  4. GNSS / L-band / satellite-to-phone filtered LNAs. Our SAW (QPQ106x) with our LNAs in a 2×1.6 mm package.

    have it · vs 20×18 mm modules (Taoglas); low price per unit.

  5. UWB filtered LNA, channels 5 and 9. QM14068 switch-LNA plus a channel filter, sold with our UWB chips.

    filter gap · one BAW filter per channel may work (est); both channels in one filter need LTCC.

2. Where else we see opportunities

  • Automotive coexistence (Wi-Fi, C-V2X, GNSS): parts exist (SKY5A100x, QPQ2200Q); AEC-Q keeps newcomers out.
  • Radio-altimeter and C-band earth-station protection (3.98–4.4 GHz): US retrofit by 2030; fits Trans-Tech ceramic and Lark cavity filters.
  • 6G FR3 (7–8 GHz) and mmWave IF filters: BAW for FR3 already sampling; timing ~2029+ (est).
  • X-band chain (limiter + filter + LNA): we have every block except an X-band filter; crowded, low volume.
  • BizDev demand: RF filter blocks are needed in 25 S-band, 22 C-band and 6 each X- and Ku-band applications. At X and Ku, only competitors serve them today.

3. Market trend

  • Filters are moving into modules. Most filter value sits inside FEMs; BAW is ~91 % Broadcom + Qorvo. Standalone 1–11 GHz is likely low billions (est).
  • New bands drive new sockets: Wi-Fi 7/8 (~118 M APs in 2026, unverified), US upper C-band auction (2027), UWB (~1.4 B devices by 2030), FR3.
  • Wideband catalogue filters are commodity. LTCC sells at ~$4 or less; Mini-Circuits lists ~110 active filters at 4–11 GHz.
  • Fewer acoustic rivals: Akoustis exited in 2025; XBAR (Murata) is the main new wideband acoustic technology.

Risks

  • Post-merger cost cuts could prune low-volume standalone parts; Qorvo's SAW fab transfer is under way.
  • Filter market figures differ 2–3× between sources.
  • Qorvo data gaps: about 33 duplexers and the newest parts are not yet in BizDev; a search for them is running.
