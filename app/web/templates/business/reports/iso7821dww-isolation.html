Reinforced digital isolators for power, charging and cars: TI ISO7821DWW and our 8 mm limit

21 Oct 2026 · Research idea for a business case · built from BizDev data (parts database for TI, Skyworks and Novosense digital isolators, cross results, block-diagram library with 219 application diagrams, TI reference designs, library topic on traction-inverter isolation), plus public application notes on creepage and pollution degree and TI's public list prices (sections 3 and 4). Open points are marked “open”.

In brief

  1. The part: TI ISO7821DWW is a 2-channel (one each way) reinforced digital isolator in a 16-pin extra-wide SOIC (DWW): 5.7 kVrms, 2828 Vpk working voltage, 14.5 mm creepage, 100 Mbps, 85 kV/µs CMTI, –55 to 125 °C, industrial grade, enable pins on both sides.
  2. Our position: our closest crosses, Si86S621 and Si86S622 (SSO-8), are reinforced too and better on CMTI (100 kV/µs), isolation rating (6 kVrms) and speed (150 Mbps), with automotive (-AS4) versions. But none of our 672 digital isolators passes 8 mm creepage; TI has 20 parts and Novosense 6 at 14.5–15 mm.
  3. Where it matters: digital isolators sit in 66 of the 219 application diagrams, among them the HEV/EV on-board charger, DC fast charger, AC wallboxes, the HV-to-LV DC/DC and traction inverters, next to our isolated gate drivers, isolated DC-DC and isolated amplifiers.
  4. What 800 V needs (section 4): in a sealed housing (pollution degree 2), with group I mould compound like ours, a reinforced barrier needs 8.0 mm at 800 V and 10 mm at 1000 V. So 8 mm is just enough at 800 V, and too short for a fully charged 800 V battery (~900 V) or a 1000 V system. Where condensation or conductive dust is expected (pollution degree 3), even basic insulation needs 10–12.5 mm, so only the 14.5 mm class works.
  5. Price: TI asks no premium on reels (ISO7821DWWR $2.60 vs ISO7821DWR $2.64 at 1k) and +23 % in tubes ($4.12 vs $3.34). TI sells the extra-wide package at wide-body prices.
  6. The business-case question: should we offer an extra-wide package for our existing isolator die (and the neighbours) for 900–1000 V and dirty-environment designs, priced like our wide body? Market size is open.

What could combine into a product, strongest first

  1. Offer Si86S621/622-AS4 where 8 mm is enough. Better CMTI, kVrms and speed, automotive grade, today. Different package and pinout (8 vs 16 pins): a board change, not a drop-in.
  2. An extra-wide-package version of our 2-channel isolator. Same die in a 14.5 mm-class package, for reinforced barriers above 800 V (fully charged 800 V batteries, 1000 V chargers, BMS and HV junction boxes) and for basic barriers at pollution degree 3. TI sells it at wide-body reel prices, so it must not cost more to make. Open: package availability, cost and qualification time. The barrier also needs a higher rating (TI: 2000 Vrms working in DWW vs 1500 Vrms in DW).
  3. An isolation set for the charger and inverter: isolator + Si829x / Si8285/86 gate drivers + Si89xx sensing + isolated bias, sold as one reference. Open: a reinforced isolated bias controller is missing.

3. Market trend

  • Crowded and price-driven: TI, ADI, Infineon, ST, onsemi, Novosense, Chipanalog, 2Pai, Toshiba and Rohm; “established leaders plus fast-growing Chinese suppliers; increasingly price-competitive”.
  • Higher voltage: 800 V SiC is now mainstream in traction (library topic), with CMTI of 100–200 kV/µs and reinforced isolation; creepage and working-voltage needs rise with it: 8 mm reinforced at 800 V, 10 mm at 1000 V (section 4). TI names BMS, high-voltage junction boxes and heaters beyond 800 V as applications that need more than 8 mm.
  • Wide packages as a differentiator: in BizDev only TI (20 parts) and Novosense (6, e.g. NSI8321W1 at 15 mm and 7.5 kVrms) reach 14.5–15 mm.
  • Prices (TI store, 21 Oct 2026, 1k units): ISO7821DWWR $2.60 and ISO7821DWR $2.64 (reels); ISO7821DWW $4.12 and ISO7821DW $3.34 (tubes). Open: market size and volumes; Novosense prices.

4. Creepage, pollution degree and what 800 V systems need

Creepage is the distance along the package surface between the two sides. The minimum depends on three things (IEC 60664-1): the working voltage (rms or DC), how dirty and damp the air gets (pollution degree) and how well the surface resists tracking (material group, from the CTI of the mould compound). Reinforced insulation needs twice the basic distance.

  • PD1: no dirt, or only dry dirt (sealed, potted or coated). PD2: the dirt does not conduct, but now and then condensation makes it conduct (closed housings, offices; most isolator datasheets and sealed car and charger boxes). PD3: conductive dirt, or condensation is expected (factory floors, unheated rooms, the outside of outdoor boxes). PD4: lasting wetness or conductive dust (open outdoors).
  • Material group: I (CTI ≥ 600), II (400–600), IIIa (175–400, most FR4 boards), IIIb (100–175). TI and Skyworks isolator packages are group I, which halves the distance compared with group III.

What each application designs to

  • EV traction inverter and BMS: 400 V or 800 V battery (~900 V when full, 1000 V design ceiling, est); PD2 inside the sealed housing; reinforced between the 12 V side and HV.
  • On-board charger: PD2, reinforced. TI's worked example needs 4 mm at 400 V and 8 mm at 800 V (group I), and 11.2 mm for group II.
  • DC fast charger and solar inverter: up to ~1000 V DC (solar also 1500 V); PD3 outside, PD2 inside a sealed IP65 cabinet (from a TÜV report for a Sungrow inverter; for chargers est).
  • Industrial motor drive (IEC 61800-5-1): 380–480 V mains and a 565–800 V DC bus; PD2 in a cabinet, PD3 on machine tools; basic to earth, reinforced to I/O that people can touch.
  • Standards: car standards (ISO 6469-3, ISO 21498, LV 123) set voltage classes and tests, not creepage; the creepage comes from IEC 60664-1. Component standards (IEC 60747-17 / VDE 0884-17, UL 1577) rate the barrier inside the package, not the creepage the system needs.

8 mm or 14.5 mm for 800 V

  • 8 mm is enough for basic insulation at PD2 up to 1000 V, for reinforced at PD2 up to 800 V, and for anything coated or potted (PD1: 4.8 mm reinforced at 800 V). The JEDEC wide SOIC-16 gives only ~7.6 mm in air (AN583), so our SSO-8 (8.0 mm) fits better.
  • 14.5 mm is needed for reinforced insulation at PD2 above ~800 V (900 V needs 9 mm, 1000 V needs 10 mm) and for basic insulation at PD3 (10–12.5 mm).
  • No single package is enough for reinforced insulation at PD3 (20–25 mm). Designers seal the box (PD2), coat or pot the board (PD1), or put two isolators in series.
  • Design measures that relax it: conformal coating or potting, a sealed housing, the reinforced barrier only where people can touch (basic or functional isolation at the drivers), and board slots (at least 1 mm wide at PD2, 1.5 mm at PD3) so the FR4 board keeps the package's distance.

Risks

  • Most sealed 400 V and 800 V designs are fine with 8 mm at PD2. More than 8 mm is needed only for reinforced barriers above ~800 V, at PD3, or where coating is not wanted (the share of such designs is open).
  • TI sells DWW reels at DW prices, so an extra-wide package earns no price premium; it only wins sockets.
  • A new package means qualification time and cost against a price-driven market.
  • Our working voltage (2121 Vpk) is below ISO7821DWW's (2828 Vpk), and our low-temperature end is –40 °C vs –55 °C.
