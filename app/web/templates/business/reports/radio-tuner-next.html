Car radio: a radio node for the antenna (2–3 years, reuse only)

Business-case draft, 20 Oct 2026 · Not reviewed · Built from the BizDev block-diagram library, our broadcast data (Si479xx tuners, Si46xx decoders) and about 140 public web requests. Confidence: high = primary document read; med = trade press or an advocacy body; low = search snippet or analyst press release. “est” = our estimate. All figures, prices and sources are in the background notes.

In brief

  1. Our advantage is the architecture, not one chip. Our radio is split into chainable tuners and a separate multi-standard decoder, so it can sit at the antenna, in the head unit, or hand raw samples to a central computer. NXP builds everything into one head-unit chip; ST hands everything to the main processor. Cars are moving to zonal architectures, where RF stays at the antenna and audio moves to the central computer: that favours our split.
  2. Our disadvantages: higher cost per chip than ST’s mature reused designs, more chips than NXP’s one-chip radio, a non-standard sample output and weaker software-radio partnerships. At the antenna, a module built from our parts needs 5–7 parts.
  3. Proposal: the radio node. One part with 2 or 4 of our tuners, our multi-standard decoder and a direct car-network output (decoded audio and data, or raw samples for a central software radio). It reuses our tuner and decoder circuits; the network output is the new part. Target volume price about $16–19 for 4 tuners and $10–12 for 2 (est), replacing about $22–29 of today’s parts.

Supporting actions (existing parts, no new silicon)

  • AMIC on every tuner: firmware and licence on the single tuners that lack it (Si4790x, Si4791x, Si4792x, Si4795x); also as software for central software radios.
  • Remote tuner module reference design today: 2 × Si47962 + Si469xx over A²B, to win module design-ins before the node exists.
  • CDR to production for China: release Si46933/Si46943; first confirm which tuners support CDR (our data lists none).
  • Document the existing I/Q output for software-radio processors (the 2017 Renesas R-Car design shows it works).

8. Raising the attach rate

Two meanings: how often we win the radio, and how many other Skyworks parts come with it.

  1. Module reference design with antenna makers. Customers value pre-validated, homologated modules that “deploy within weeks” (Hirschmann). Start now with today’s parts; move to the node. med
  2. Partner listings with radio-software companies. OpenSynergy already supports us (not ST); Inntot is in ST’s partner programme, not ours. Tested ports for the sample mode. low–med
  3. Fast access for partners: a quick standard NDA or partner programme with documentation, drivers and kits for software companies and processor makers. Today the automotive kit is NDA-gated through sales. low
  4. Certification support: pre-tested HD Radio (Xperi/Intertek), DAB and DRM results and EV noise tests in the reference module. med
  5. Software continuity: keep the common programming interface of Si479xx and Si46xx so existing radio software runs on the node. design rule
  6. Simple licensing and long-term supply: one licence price list (HD per channel, MRC, AMIC); a published longevity commitment (none found today). low
  7. Bundle more Skyworks content: clocks, power and antenna-module RF parts in the reference module; the BizDev configurator and block-diagram editor produce the customer bill of materials. low

9. Risks and open questions

  • Process: can tuner and decoder circuits share one die? Our data doesn’t say; step 1 (one package) avoids the question.
  • Network output: A²B, Ethernet or both, and which Ethernet speed; ask module makers and the carmakers’ zonal teams.
  • Remote tuner module volumes: no public numbers or carmaker awards; the weak scenario keeps the radio in the head unit, where NXP is strong.
  • Cost: ST’s mature parts are likely cheaper per function; the node competes on system cost (fewer parts at the antenna), not per-chip cost.
  • Cannibalisation: the node replaces sales of today’s tuner + decoder sets in modules.
  • Data gaps: CDR support on our tuners, temperature ranges, contract prices, RF performance and cost versus ST and NXP. nxp.com, st.com, sec.gov and investors.skyworksinc.com blocked automated access.
