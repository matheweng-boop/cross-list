Car-radio tuners: what to build next (2–3 years, reuse only)

Business-case draft, 20 Oct 2026 · Not reviewed · Built from BizDev broadcast data (Si479xx tuners, Si46xx coprocessors and receivers) and about 140 public web requests. Confidence: high = primary document read; med = trade press or an advocacy body; low = search snippet or analyst press release. “est” = our estimate. All figures, prices and sources are in the background notes.

In brief

  1. The radio is splitting into an RF front end and software. Decoding is moving into the cockpit processor (software-defined radio, SDR) or into a remote tuner module (RTM) near the antenna. Every route still needs a tuner per antenna path; the separate decoder chip is what is at risk.
  2. Build first: a dual digital-output tuner, “Si4796x-D”. It reuses the Si47962 chip and the L-band block of Si47912, drops the audio pins (as Si47942 already does) and adds a standard digital I/Q output. It answers ST’s STA710 and keeps us in SDR and RTM designs. Target volume price about $5–6.50 (est).
  3. Quick wins around it:
    • AMIC on all single tuners (firmware only), and as SoC software;
    • an RTM reference design from today’s parts;
    • CDR to production for China.
    Then: a quad tuner (two dies in one package) and a tuner + DRM/CDR coprocessor in one package for China and India. The cheapest lever of all: open the NDA-gated evaluation kit and documentation.

Device concepts

Specs come from our own data shorts (file and section in the background notes). Effort is relative (low / med / high) with reasons; no cost or person-month numbers are invented.

1. Si4796x-D: dual digital-output tuner build first

ReusesSi47962 die: dual AM/FM/LW/SW/WB and Band III front ends, PLLs, radio DSP (phase diversity, RDS, DAB noise canceller, digital AGC, AMIC, band scan), ZIF I/Q, loop-through. L-band block from Si47912. The audio-free QFN-56 pin map of Si47942
RemovesAudio DAC and I2S audio pins (10 pins freed). Note: Si4796x has no audio DSP to remove
AddsA standard digital I/Q output on the freed pins; L-band (1452–1492 MHz) on a dual die; firmware for raw-I/Q streaming and for exporting both tuners to host PD/MRC
BandsFM 64–108 MHz, AM 520–1710 kHz, LW 144–288 kHz, SW 2.3–30 MHz, weather band, DAB Band III 168–240 MHz, L-band 1452–1492 MHz (new)
I/Q servedHD (744.1875 kS/s), DAB (2.048 MS/s), DRM (192 kS/s); CDR rate to be confirmed
Output optionsA today’s ZIF pins re-framed as standard I2S/TDM: no new pins, low effort. B LVDS I2S on 4 differential pairs: med effort. C JESD204B: about 7–8 pins and a new high-speed SerDes, high effort. ST offers all three; we should offer at least A and B
PackageQFN-56, 8 × 8 mm: fits the existing QFN-56 dual layout (as Si47942 does), but not pin-compatible with Si4796x where the audio pins were used
Grade, supplyAEC-Q100 A-grade (as the base; temperature range not in our data); 3.3 V analog, 1.8 V digital, 1.8/3.3 V I/O
AnswersST STA710 (dual, AM/FM/WX/DAB incl. L-band, I2S/LVDS/JESD204B). Advantage for us: phase diversity and AMIC on chip, pin/API family compatibility
EffortMed with output A or B; med–high with L-band; high with JESD204B

2. Si4799x-Q: quad digital-output tuner second

Two Si4796x-D dies in one package, linked inside the package (shared clock, IC link and boot flash, loop-through between cores). Enables phase diversity + background scan + DAB MRC from one part, which needs two ICs today. Answers NXP SAF4000 (up to 4 tuners) and partly SAF9000 (5). Effort: med as a two-die package; high as a single four-tuner die (no precedent beyond 2 tuners per die). Not layout-compatible with today’s boards.

3. AMIC everywhere quick win

Enable AMIC (licence SW479-AMIC-GE01) on the single tuners that lack it (Si4790x, Si4791x, Si4792x, Si4795x). AMIC already runs on single tuners (Si4798x, Si47992), so this is firmware and licence work: low effort (med for Si4790x if its DSP has no headroom; not stated). Then AMIC-SW: a port to cockpit SDR stacks, med effort. Answers NXP EVAM-lite.

4. RTM reference design quick win

Decoded RTM from today’s parts: 2 × Si47962 (4 tuners) + Si469xx coprocessor, audio and data to the head unit over A²B (fits the coprocessor’s I2S/TDM directly) or Ethernet (needs a bridge processor). No new silicon, low–med effort. An I/Q RTM for central SDR follows once Si4796x-D exists.

5. China and India: CDR to production, then a tuner + coprocessor package near

Release Si46933/Si46943 (CDR, 3/4 channels, now Preview): low–med effort. First confirm which tuners are qualified for CDR I/Q; our data lists none. Then “Si4796x-P” (P for package): Si47967 (dual AM/FM + DRM, phase diversity, AMIC) and a 2-channel Si469xx in one package, no die change, med effort; replaces two packages in cost-sensitive head units.

6. Automotive single-chip DAB+ receiver later

Requalify Si468x (today −40 to 85 °C, no AEC-Q100), low–med effort, or add audio to Si4629 (already AEC-Q100, no audio output), med effort. A cost play for EU entry radios, where DAB+ is mandatory.

Not recommended in 2–3 years

  • An NXP SAF9000-style radio + audio + AI SoC: needs a new DSP, an NPU and a larger process.
  • A new standalone audio DSP: audio is moving into the cockpit processor too.

Raising the attach rate

Two meanings: how often our tuner wins the socket, and how many other Skyworks parts come with it. Ranked by likely effect against effort.

  1. Open the evaluation path. Today the automotive kit (Si4699X-3DTF2-EVB) has its documents under NDA through sales, while consumer kits are open. Publish basic documentation and drivers; keep advanced features under NDA. low effort
  2. “Works with” software bundles. OpenSynergy’s tuner SDK supports our Si479xx and Si46xx (and NXP, not ST). Inntot is in ST’s partner programme, not ours. Join or start partner listings with Inntot/Visteon, OpenSynergy and M’s STYLE, with tested reference ports. low–med
  3. SDR and RTM reference designs. A cockpit-processor board (Renesas R-Car precedent from 2017; add Qualcomm-class) and the RTM reference, both with layout, bill of materials and test results. Hirschmann shows what customers value: pre-validated, homologated, “deploy within weeks”. med
  4. Keep footprints and software compatible. Si4794x/5x/6x are already pin, package and API compatible. Make Si4796x-D fit the same QFN-56 layout and keep the common API. design rule
  5. Certification support. HD Radio certification goes through Xperi with Intertek testing. Offer pre-tested reference results and a certification guide; do the same for DAB/DRM conformance and EV noise tests. med
  6. Simple licensing. One price list for HD per channel, MRC and AMIC, visible with the kit. low
  7. Long-term supply statement. None found for our broadcast line; a published longevity commitment answers OEM concerns. low
  8. Bundle more Skyworks content. Reference designs that also use our clocks, power and antenna-module RF parts; the BizDev radio configurator and block-diagram editor give a customer-specific bill of materials. low

Risks and open questions

  • Output interface: which of I2S/TDM, LVDS or JESD204B do Renesas, Qualcomm-class SoCs and Visteon/Inntot accept? Decide before tape-out.
  • Coprocessor erosion: SDR and I/Q RTMs remove Si469xx sockets over time; AMIC/MRC licences and regional packages offset part of it.
  • NXP SAF9000: if it reaches volume at a good price, stand-alone tuners narrow to non-NXP platforms.
  • Regulation timing: US AM bill (Senate), China standard, India’s FM standard all pending.
  • Data gaps: CDR support on our tuners, temperature ranges, DSP headroom for AMIC on Si4790x, the L-band pin of Si47912, and all contract prices. Several competitor facts rest on search snippets because nxp.com, st.com, sec.gov and investors.skyworksinc.com blocked automated access.
