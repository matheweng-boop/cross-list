How this is measured, and its limits
  • Parts: {{ '{:,}'.format(n_parts) }} Skyworks and Qorvo catalogue parts in these functions. A part covers a band when its published frequency range overlaps it (IEEE letter bands). {{ '{:,}'.format(no_freq) }} state no readable frequency and are left out, not guessed.
  • Power amplifiers are split by the highest output power stated (P1dB, Psat, linear or rated output power): up to 1 W, 1–10 W, over 10 W.
  • Demand counts library and background applications whose blocks need the function in that band. For {{ n_stated }} applications the band (and for power amplifiers the output power) is stated per block, with its reason (data/business/rf_block_bands.yaml: from the vendor sources, or the usual allocation where they say nothing); the others fall back to rules on the application's name (data/business/rf_bands.yaml). {{ unknown }} of {{ n_rf }} RF applications have no band at all.
  • Competitors: {{ '{:,}'.format(n_comp) }} active RF parts from {{ comp_makers|join(', ') if comp_makers else 'no maker yet' }}, collected from their public product pages. They are counted to show who else serves a slot ("competitors only" = proven demand; "crowded" = {{ 4 }} or more makers) and are never offered as our parts. Makers whose sites block automated access are missing, so "nobody" means nobody in BizDev.
