What it is

Common-mode transient immunity is the fastest rate of change of the voltage across the isolation barrier that the part tolerates without an output upset, under the datasheet's test conditions. It is quoted in kV/µs (1 kV/µs = 1 V/ns). The guaranteed minimum is what counts; typical values are not a design limit.

Why it matters

Required CMTI ≈ ΔV / Δt of the switch node. An 800 V bus switching in 20 ns produces 40 kV/µs: a 50 kV/µs part has 1.25× margin, a 150 kV/µs part 3.75×. Too little margin means either slowing the edges (gate resistors, softer turn-on: more switching loss and heat) or risking upsets: output glitches, corrupted data, false faults and, in gate drivers, possible shoot-through. The mechanism differs between gate drivers, digital isolators, current sensors and isolated power, but the margin check is the same.

Higher CMTI does not by itself improve efficiency or EMI. It gives the freedom to switch faster without upsets. If both parts hold the required margin at the real slew rate, the higher rating brings no functional advantage there.
