What it is

Pulse-width distortion is the difference between the rising- and falling-edge delays of one channel; it stretches or shrinks pulses. Skew is the delay difference between channels (or between parts).

Why it matters

In a half bridge the commanded dead time must cover the worst-case timing mismatch between the high- and low-side paths: tdead,min ≥ toff,max − ton,min + the power device's own margin. Tighter timing allows a shorter dead time, which reduces dead-time distortion and diode conduction. Reducing dead time from 300 ns to 200 ns at 20 kHz changes the dead-time fraction from 0.6 % to 0.4 % of the period: a timing gain, not a 0.2 % efficiency gain.

Not modelled The dead-time budget needs minimum and maximum delays of both paths and the power device's switching behaviour; we store only the maximum delay so far.
