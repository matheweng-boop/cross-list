Channel numbers and centres are those of IEEE 802.15.4z (HRP UWB, 499.2 MHz channels). The Car Connectivity Consortium (CCC) says channel 5 "will soon be rendered useless" by Wi-Fi and IMT and that channel 9 is "the only global channel" left for the digital key.

What goes wrong

  • In-band interference. A Wi-Fi 6E/7 transmitter puts tens of dB more power per MHz on channel 5 or 6 than UWB is allowed (-41.3 dBm/MHz). While Wi-Fi sends, the UWB receiver loses the packet. UWB is far below Wi-Fi's energy-detect threshold, so Wi-Fi never waits for UWB: the UWB side must avoid Wi-Fi, not the other way round (802.15.4ab coexistence study, rule d8).
  • Desense from out-of-band noise. Even on channel 9 the Wi-Fi transmitter's noise skirt and harmonics raise the UWB noise floor when the antennas are close; every dB of noise is a dB of range lost {{ est|safe }}.
  • Blocking and compression. A strong Wi-Fi or phone signal a few centimetres away can push the UWB LNA or switch into compression although it is outside the channel. ETSI EN 302 065-3-1 asks anchors to keep working with a -50 dBm interferer (rule d5); a compressed front end also distorts the pulse and the first-path timing.
  • Shared antenna fin or cabin. A roof module with Wi-Fi, V2X, cellular and a UWB or NBA antenna has only 20-30 dB of antenna-to-antenna isolation {{ est|safe }}; a phone hotspot or the car's own access point sits in the same cabin as the interior anchors.
  • The phone side. The phone (the tag) carries 6 GHz Wi-Fi and UWB in one device; the phone maker schedules them, the car cannot {{ est|safe }}.

Mitigations

  • Prefer channel 9. It is allowed in every main region and 612 MHz away from Wi-Fi; use channel 5 only as a fallback where no 6 GHz Wi-Fi is near (rule d6).
  • Filtering. A BAW filter on the Wi-Fi transmitter (SKY5A1008, 5945-7125 MHz; its rejection at UWB channel 9 is not stated) cuts the noise it puts on UWB; a channel-9 band-pass in front of the anchor's LNA rejects Wi-Fi and IMT blockers (no Skyworks or Qorvo channel-9 filter in our catalogue: a gap). For 802.15.4ab assist radios a V2X notch (QPQ2230Q, SKY5A1005) keeps the 5.9 GHz V2X transmitter out of the NBA receiver.
  • Time-domain coexistence. Short UWB ranging slots (about 1.5-5 % airtime, rule d10), a coexistence signal from the UWB chip to the Wi-Fi chip so Wi-Fi pauses during a ranging round {{ est|safe }}, and the 802.15.4ab tools: spectrum-sensing deferral (listen before talk), detect-and-avoid and channel-map exclusion (trend t6).
  • Antenna isolation and a robust front end. Separate the UWB and Wi-Fi antennas, point their nulls at each other; aim for 40 dB or more of isolation plus filter rejection {{ est|safe }}. Use an LNA with a bypass mode (QM14068) and a switch that survives strong signals (SKY5A2108, AEC-Q100 Grade 2).
